Read the two directions of trade between Iran and Russia separately. When Iran is the reporting country, imports from Russia and exports to Russia appear on separate rows. When Russia reports those same flows, it calls them exports to Iran and imports from Iran, respectively. Show which country reported each figure.

## What to record before publishing

| Field | What to publish alongside the figure |
| --- | --- |
| Period | Month and year, calendar used and date of the last update |
| Direction of trade | Reporting country, trading partner, and whether the figure covers imports or exports |
| Scope | All goods, or a specified product group with its tariff code |
| Unit | Value and currency, or weight and unit of measurement |
| Source | Link to the original table or file, and an indication if the figures are preliminary |

## Why two reports may show different figures

The reported value of imports typically includes freight and insurance to the importing country’s border. The reported value of exports is generally measured at the exporting country’s border. Differences in recording dates may also put the same goods in different months. For month-on-month comparisons, use the same reporting country and a consistent definition.

UN Comtrade updates its data as it receives official statistics from countries. If figures for one direction of trade are unavailable for the month in question, say that the data have not been published. Use zero only if the source reports zero.
