In the third chapter of the *Golestan*, the narrator spends an evening as the guest of a merchant at his shop on the island of Kish. The merchant talks of another voyage that is to be his last. He plans to take Persian sulphur to China, buy porcelain there and trade in several other markets before returning to Persia. He tells Saadi that he will give up trading after the voyage.

## Kish on the trade routes

In Saadi’s account, the merchant is describing a route he intends to take. Kish makes sense as the setting. According to *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, the island was a centre of seaborne trade in the medieval Persian Gulf, with links to markets around the Indian Ocean. That background helps explain why the *Golestan*’s readers could readily imagine a merchant in Kish talking about goods and markets far from home.

## A question worth asking today

Saadi considers the merchant’s eagerness for the next deal in a chapter on contentment. The story also raises a practical question for traders today. If a sale falls through partway along the route, how much capital remains tied up in the goods, and what obligations do we have to the next buyer? Before linking several deals together, it helps to work out the figures for each leg separately.
